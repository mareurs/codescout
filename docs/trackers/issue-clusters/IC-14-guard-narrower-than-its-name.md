## IC-14 — a guard's coverage is narrower than its name, so the name is what everyone reasons with

**Slug:** `cluster/guard-narrower-than-its-name`
**Claim:** A guard's name states the property; its implementation covers a subset of it. Everything the name promises is believed protected, the uncovered remainder is protected by nothing, and the guard's own green result is what conceals the gap.

**These seven span SEVEN DISTINCT NARROWING MECHANISMS, and that is the finding the class did not have before 2026-09-02.** Ranked by what does the narrowing: a **write/read polarity** (member 1), **traversal depth** (2), a **name filter** (3), **label parsing** (4), **directory enumeration** (5), **markup** (6), and **the axis the author chose to extend along** (7). No two share a fix. The pattern only became visible because five of the six were found in one day by one programme, which is also why the class's `n` is not the interesting number — a count of instances says nothing about how many distinct ways a guard can be narrower than its name, and that second figure is what predicts whether the next guard is safe.

1. **`worktree-guard-refuses-writes-and-lets-unpinned-reads-through`** — the guard's name describes the *ambiguity*, and it covers only the **write** half of it: an unpinned write in a worktree-bearing checkout is refused with every worktree enumerated, while an unpinned READ resolves silently against whatever the activation slot holds. What makes it the class's sharpest member is that the read failure is **not always a zero**. Measured 2026-09-02: `symbols(name="duplicate_definitions")` unpinned returned a *populated* result — `duplicate_definitions_and_citations_dedupe`, main's only substring hit — while the symbol asked for exists 7× in `.worktrees/entry-id-collision` and 0× at `experiments`. The answer was correct for the tree it searched, named no scope, and did not look like absence; a zero at least prompts doubt, and a plausible near-miss does not. Three sessions contributed three tiers of evidence and the causal half is deliberately left open — `codescout-17` corrected its own contribution rather than let a wrong-slot probe stand in for an empty-slot one, which is why the record has tiers instead of a mechanism.
2. **`param-probe-does-not-recurse-so-nesting-a-key-removes-it-from-guard-reach`** — `param_probe::sweep` walks `schema["properties"]` one level only; Task 4's fold nested nine previously top-level-probed `event_create` keys under `event`, and none of the nine are probed anymore — an unhonored key labelled for `event_create` inside `event.properties` passes the probe silently, while the identical key at top level reds it.
3. **`both-doc-citation-guards-skip-half-the-corpus-without-saying-so`** — `tests/doc_tool_refs.rs`'s two guards over documented tool calls each exclude non-matching tokens by `continue`, and their union is **304 of 618 anchored citations (49%)**, measured 2026-09-02 by replicating the scanner. `a_documented_call_names_a_live_tool` skips via `if !c.tool.contains('_') { continue; }` — a population filter that was valid when every librarian tool was snake_case and silently stopped being one when the most-cited tool was renamed to `doc`; `a_documented_tool_parameter_exists_on_that_tool` skips via `schemas.get("artifact") → None → continue`, treating a `schemas` miss as *"not a tool"* rather than *"not a **live** tool"* — which is precisely the case a liveness guard exists to catch.
